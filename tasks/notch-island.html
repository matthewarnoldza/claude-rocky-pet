Move the pet into the notch

Proposal for replacing the floating pet and its speech bubble with an island around the MacBook camera notch that expands when it has something to say.

TL;DR

Yes, the notch is the better home, and it works on your Mac. I tested it: an Electron window can be placed at the very top of the screen, over the menu bar, on your 14-inch MacBook Pro (menu bar 33 pt tall, screen 1512 pt wide).

The island fixes the three things wrong with today's bubbles: lines are capped at about 110 characters, the bubble and pills collide with the character's props, and the pet covers whatever is in that corner. In the notch it takes no screen space when idle, speech gets a full-width panel, and the session list lives in the same place instead of a separate stack.

It is a rewrite of the window and layout, not a restyle. The session tracking, hooks, alerts file and speech code stay as they are.

What this means for the artwork

TopicChange from the earlier brief
Two sizesIdle is about 26 px tall (52 px on Retina). Expanded is about 64 px tall. Art should be drawn large and checked at the small size.
BackgroundThe island is solid black. Art needs true transparency and should not rely on a dark outline to read; the sheet you shared shows frames on dark tiles, so confirm the exports are transparent.
Stills are enough to startThe sheet has one frame per state. That works: I can add motion in code (breathing, hop, squash, blink). Frame-by-frame animation can come later.
States that drop outWith "island only", walking and being dragged are not needed. The other ten states on your sheet all map directly.
Symbols beside the characterThe "!", "?" and "zzz" marks in your frames will be cramped beside the camera. Better as separate small sprites I can place, or left out of the idle size.
Copy lengthThe 110-character cap goes away. Lines of about 200 characters fit comfortably, so the model can say something more specific.

How I would build it

  1. Island shell. A fixed-size transparent window at the top centre, above the menu bar. The black island is drawn and animated inside it, so the window itself never resizes. Clicks pass through everywhere except the island.
  2. Move status and speech in. The pill list becomes rows in the expanded panel; spoken lines go to the speech area. The session, alert and speech code is reused unchanged.
  3. Character states. One image per state, chosen from the session state, with motion added in code. Placeholder art until yours arrives.
  4. Remove the floating pet (if you choose "island only"): drag, walk, sphere, hats, props and the after-hours bounce.

Steps 1 and 2 give you something to try with placeholder art before the real assets land.

Risks and open questions

CONFIRMEDPlacement works on your Mac. A test window accepted a position at the very top of the screen at three different window levels. I confirmed this from the reported position, not by eye, so the first build step is to look at it.
WARNINGThe island's side wings cover part of the menu bar. An app with many menus (or many status icons) can reach the notch, and the wings would sit on top of the last item on each side. Keeping the wings narrow, or choosing "character only" or "hidden until needed", reduces this.
WARNINGNotch width is not available to the app. Electron does not report it. I would use a measured value for the 14-inch MacBook Pro and make it a setting; other Mac models would need their own number.
WARNINGFull-screen apps. In full screen the menu bar hides and the notch area is black. The island would still show there; whether it should, or should hide until an event, is worth deciding once you see it.
INFOAuto-expanding is an interruption. A panel dropping from the top of the screen is more noticeable than a bubble in the corner. Decision 3 controls how often that happens.
INFOOpenNotch as a base. It is open source (MIT), but it is a separate full app with its own tools (clipboard, music, calendar). Adapting your pet into it would mean starting over in its codebase; borrowing the interaction pattern is cheaper.
